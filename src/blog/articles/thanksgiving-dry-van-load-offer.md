A broker offers $1,525 for 500 loaded miles, picking up Wednesday and delivering Friday. That works out to $3.05 a mile, exactly the national dry-van spot average DAT reported for October. Before you say yes, ask your dispatcher two questions. How far am I from pickup? Who confirmed the receiver is unloading Friday? [DAT’s November 10 rate report](https://www.dat.com/company/news-events/news-releases/dat-van-spot-rate-rises-to-3-05-a-mile-highest-since-early-2022)

The first answer changes what the truck earns per mile. The second can change what it earns for the week.

## Two offers for the same truck

Suppose both loads pick up Wednesday, run 500 loaded miles and deliver into the same freight market. These are illustrative offers. Both quotes include fuel.

| | Load A | Load B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Broker’s offer | $1,525 | $1,450 |
| Dollars per loaded mile | $3.05 | $2.90 |
| Empty miles to pickup | 100 | 20 |
| Total miles to delivery | 600 | 520 |
| Gross revenue per total mile | $2.54 | $2.79 |

Load A pays $75 more to drive 80 extra miles. In effect, the broker is offering about 94 cents for each extra mile. At an illustrative $6 per gallon and 6 miles per gallon, those miles alone burn $80 in diesel. You have already spent the extra $75 before counting tires or the time it took to reach the shipper.

If Load B is still available and you want Load A to match its gross rate on every mile, the counter is **$1,674 all-in**. Take $1,450, divide by 520 miles, multiply by 600 and round up to the next dollar. That gives the dispatcher a number they can explain. Your own costs and hours still determine whether either offer works.

## What exactly does “Friday delivery” mean?

Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26 this year. TQL flagged holiday closures and changes to shipping schedules in its November freight outlook. The distinction that matters on a Wednesday call is whether the receiver has actually issued a Friday appointment. A date typed on the offer gives you little to work with if the facility closes until Monday. [TQL’s November market report](https://www.tql.com/about-us/tql-freight-insights/november-2026/tql-november-market-report-trucking-capacity-keeps-rates-elevated)

Ask for the appointment number, receiving hours in local time and whether the facility will unload you or accept a dropped trailer. If Friday falls through, find out the next available unloading time. Ask the broker to put any agreed layover pay, eligibility terms and documentation requirements on the rate confirmation. Until those terms are agreed, use zero layover revenue in the comparison.

Then check the next pickup against the time your truck will actually be empty. A Friday morning reload is worth leaving room for only if you can unload, reach the next shipper and make its cutoff with the hours you have left.

## The call to make before booking

Here’s how the dispatcher could put Load A back to the broker.

“With my truck 100 miles out, I can cover this at $1,674 all-in. Can you send the Friday appointment number and receiving hours? If the receiver moves us to Monday, what layover terms can you put on the confirmation?”

The owner-operator now has a total price, a mileage estimate from the truck’s current location and a time when the truck can earn again. If you’re the one behind the wheel, ask dispatch to text you those three numbers together while both offers are still on the board.
